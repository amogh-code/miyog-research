# Overview

## Research question

Can a discovery system be organised so that the components that propose explanations never decide their standing? MIYOG answers this for equational laws in finite formal worlds, and it measures what that separation costs and where it stops.

## Scientific intelligence as an operational term

The paper uses *scientific intelligence* (SI) for a set of evidential obligations. It does not mean a new kind of intelligence. A system meets the obligations to the extent that it:

1. distinguishes questions of probability, possibility, necessity, impossibility and explanation;
2. attaches to each answer the kind of evidence that supports it;
3. searches for witnesses that would overturn claims of necessity;
4. chooses experiments that discriminate between competing models;
5. revises conclusions that depended on a premise later shown false.

Claims of necessity are conditional on a model, its definitions and a stated domain. The paper does not claim that MIYOG meets these obligations in general.

## Scope

All environments are small formal worlds written or generated by the author. A law checked over a finite domain is checked over that domain only. A proved law is proved from the definitions of a formal world. Nothing here is a statement about physical systems.
